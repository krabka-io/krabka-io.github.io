# Krabka trademark policy

This policy covers the Krabka name, logo, and wordmark, including the published
brand lockups. It distinguishes permission to use a mark from permission to
copy the artwork. It does not claim that a mark is registered or identify a
new owner.

## References to the project

You may use the Krabka name and the unmodified published marks to identify
and accurately refer to the Krabka project. You may describe compatibility
with Krabka when the description is accurate and does not imply endorsement.
Keep the surrounding text clear about who provides your product or service.

Do not imply endorsement, official status, sponsorship, or affiliation that
does not exist. Do not use a Krabka mark as the mark of your own product or
service. Use the published marks without restyling or recoloring them.

## Forks and derivative products

Give a fork or derivative product its own name and branding. You may state
that it is based on Krabka, but make clear that it is a separate project.
Do not present a modified distribution as an official Krabka release or use
the marks in a way that confuses users about its source.

## Other branding uses

Ask the project maintainers for permission before a branding use outside the
permissions above. Describe the proposed use through the project's normal
contact channels; this policy does not create a new contact address.
Nothing in this policy restricts lawful descriptive use or other rights that
do not require trademark permission.

## Copyright is separate

The original brand artwork retains its Apache-2.0 copyright license, as the
existing brand page states. This includes the logos, wordmark, lockups,
favicons, and generated previews. Read the
[license scope](LICENSE#brand-artwork-and-trademarks) and the complete
[Apache-2.0 terms](LICENSES/Apache-2.0.txt).

The open-source copyright licenses do not grant trademark rights. These
trademark conditions are not additional restrictions on the Apache-2.0
copyright license. A use that is permitted under copyright law can still
require separate trademark permission.
